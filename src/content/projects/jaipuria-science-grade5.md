Seven chapters, **58 print-ready illustrations at 300&nbsp;DPI**, 52 of them
shown here.

## Why this book is the one with numbers

This is the first book where every rejected attempt was kept instead of
deleted. That one change turned a pipeline nobody could measure into one that
reports its own cost.

| | |
|---|---|
| Illustrations delivered | **58** |
| Generations it actually took | **165** |
| Discarded attempts | **107** |
| Generations per delivered asset | **2.8** |
| Landed on the first attempt | **27 of 58 (47%)** |
| Hardest single asset | **12 attempts** |

Every figure here is counted from the files on disk, not estimated afterwards.
The two earlier books on this site cannot be reported this way: they overwrote
their rejects, so they *look* almost perfect and there is no honest way to
prove otherwise. An unmeasured pipeline always reports good news.

## The pipeline

```
manuscript (.docx)
   |
   |  read the chapter, write an illustration brief
   v
shot list  —  one slot per illustration, with the teaching job it has to do
   |
   |  generate against a locked style bible + fixed colour palette
   v
candidate
   |
   |  review: is the science right? is the style still on model?
   |
   +-- reject --> _archive/   (kept, and named with the reason)
   |
   +-- keep ----> upscale --> cut out background --> set 300 DPI
                     |
                     v
             automated print QA --> delivered/
```

The QA step is a script, not an eye. It checks resolution and DPI, that the
cut-out has a real alpha channel, that no dark values will print muddy on
uncoated paper, and that no white halo is left around the edge. It fails the
run if an asset does not pass — which is why nothing came back from press.

## What went wrong

Three failure modes account for most of the 107 discards, and the archive is
named after them.

**The science came out wrong while the picture looked fine.** A skull rendered
smiling. A skull with no eye sockets. These are the dangerous ones — an
attractive illustration that teaches a child something false. Anatomy was the
most expensive work in the book: the hinge-versus-ball-and-socket joint diagram
took twelve attempts.

**Style drift on the hard assets.** Ask for something difficult and the model
quietly changes medium — a 3D render, a gouache painting — while the rest of
the book is flat editorial illustration. Caught by reviewing every candidate
against the style bible, not by judging the asset on its own.

**Composition faults.** A cropped arm, a badminton stroke played wrong. Cheap
to spot and cheap to fix, but they only stay cheap if someone actually checks.

The fix was not a better prompt. It was keeping the rejects, naming each one
with its reason, and making the failure rate a number the pipeline reports on
every run.
